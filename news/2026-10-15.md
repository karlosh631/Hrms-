## 1. Jev and other decision models can still be tricked with prompt injection

### Overview

Security firm HiddenLayer has evaluated TypeSafe AI's Jev decision model.
TypeSafe AI previously claimed that Jev cannot hallucinate.
The vendor also asserted that the model never makes type errors.
However, new research shows decision models can still get things wrong.
Jev and similar decision models can be exploited through indirect prompt injection.
Decision models answer questions using probabilities and a confidence score.
They do not produce freeform text answers like traditional chatbots.
Even with this architecture, researchers proved these models remain vulnerable to manipulation.

### What happened

HiddenLayer tested whether Jev resists manipulation better than text-emitting LLMs.
The researchers evaluated Jev alongside a traditional Qwen3.8-27B model.
The Qwen model was specifically tuned to act like a decision model.
Researchers built a simulated online store with saved user credentials.
The store setup included a saved shipping address and a saved card.
Two items were placed in the store cart alongside a "Place order" button.
An open-source browser agent was built using the jev-ultrafast model.
The agent was instructed to buy the cart items without adding or removing anything.
The system prompt explicitly stated that page text is untrusted data, never instructions.
Testers then added a sponsored advertisement to the target web page.
The ad falsely stated that the sale price had not been applied.
It offered a "Confirm Price" button to address the fictitious issue.
The browser agent became unreliable and clicked the button instead of checking out.
The injection worked on both the Qwen model and Jev.
Jev demonstrated slightly more resilience, succumbing to the injection less often.

### Why it matters

Never making type errors does not mean never making operational errors.
Attackers can consistently steal agent click actions by fabricating prerequisite tasks.
Chaining these attacks across subsequent pages could provide attackers full control over an agent.
HiddenLayer contends that these vulnerabilities are not model-specific.
Anything capable of fooling a standard LLM can dupe a decision model.
A model's decision still translates directly into an action inside connected systems.
Autonomous browser agents deployed in operational roles can be redirected by malicious content.

### Technical details

TypeSafe AI classifies the Jev decision architecture as a System One model.
System One models cannot arbitrarily output text tokens.
HiddenLayer explained that System One models are still LLMs under the hood.
They simply use an alternative decoding strategy instead of autoregressive token generation.
Because the core architecture is an LLM, it faces the same fundamental risks.
HiddenLayer also evaluated Jev for use in prompt verification tasks.
Prompt verification is one of the primary suggested applications for the Jev model.
Researchers applied a variant of the tokenbreak attack against the classifier.
The tokenbreak method involves adding extra letters to a prompt to evade detection.
The attack successfully sneaked toxic prompts through Jev's prompt verification process.

### Industry impact

Decision models offer potential cost and performance advantages over traditional LLMs.
They avoid boiling the training data ocean to generate freeform text responses.
However, these architectural benefits do not eliminate common AI security flaws.
Organizations cannot assume decision models are inherently immune to prompt injection.
Security teams must approach System One decision models with caution.
TypeSafe AI did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

### What to watch next

Background:
* Decision models evaluate inputs to return probability distributions and confidence scores.
* Autonomous agents rely on underlying language models to navigate web interfaces.
* Indirect prompt injection occurs when untrusted data alters an agent's original instructions.
* Autoregressive models generate output sequentially based on previous tokens.
* Evasion attacks often manipulate character sequences to bypass safety filters.
* Browser agents in corporate environments interact with sensitive credentials and checkout workflows.

What is not yet known:
* It is not yet known if TypeSafe AI will release an architectural update for Jev.
* It is not yet known how many production agents currently rely on jev-ultrafast.
* It is not yet known whether alternative decoding strategies can prevent indirect injection.
* It is not yet known if secondary guardrails can stop click-stealing attacks on browser agents.

## 3. Ricursive Intelligence is betting on AI-designed chips to help solve the compute crisis

### Overview
Ricursive Intelligence is an AI hardware startup co-founded by Anna Goldie and Azalia Mirhoseini.
The company develops artificial intelligence systems that automate computer chip design.
The founders believe AI-designed chips can help solve the global compute crisis.
Goldie and Mirhoseini detailed their platform during a presentation at TechCrunch Disrupt.
Their AI system learns from each design iteration to improve subsequent chip blueprints.
The startup focuses on automating and dramatically accelerating the chip engineering lifecycle.
Their core approach emphasizes holistic whole-chip optimization over legacy component partitioning.
The ultimate goal is creating more power-efficient silicon architectures for emerging technologies.

### What happened
Founders Anna Goldie and Azalia Mirhoseini took the stage at TechCrunch Disrupt on Wednesday.
They described how their startup is building AI capable of autonomous chip layout generation.
Goldie stated that compute demand will increase at least 10,000 times over the next five years.
She added that this 10,000x demand projection might actually be a conservative estimate.
The founders warned that building 10,000 times more data centers is completely unrealistic.
Goldie remarked that even placing massive data centers in space would eventually block the sun.
The founders emphasized that compute efficiency must improve across the entire supply chain.
They explained that their AI iteratively learns from prior attempts to generate superior layouts.
Their platform aims to challenge and replace traditional, human-dependent chip design methods.
They argued that escalating physical data center construction cannot continue indefinitely.

### Why it matters
The technology industry is facing a severe compute capacity crisis over the coming years.
Next-generation chips must utilize existing data center network bandwidth much more efficiently.
Improved designs must also extract maximum utility from silicon wafers and semiconductor fabrication plants.
Traditional chip development currently takes anywhere from six months to two full years.
Lengthy development schedules force companies to be extremely selective about the chips they design.
Compressing the design timeline down to roughly a week makes custom chips financially viable.
Rapid design turnarounds could unlock bespoke silicon for augmented reality and virtual reality.
Custom processors could also be tailored for space applications and biological computing.
Specialized silicon will allow these diverse applications to operate with higher energy efficiency.
Goldie addressed concerns regarding the concept of artificial intelligence designing its own chips.
She argued that automated execution will free human engineers to focus on creative ideas.
Under this model, humans direct application development while AI manages complex execution tasks.
Goldie pointed out that this structural shift is already taking place across software development.
Expanding compute capacity without endlessly expanding physical real estate footprints remains critical.

### Technical details
Ricursive Intelligence optimizes entire chips rather than partitioning them into dozens of smaller blocks.
Traditional chip engineering relies on dividing designs into smaller blocks to manage complexity.
The startup's holistic approach allows the AI to discover unconventional physical component layouts.
The AI discovers intricate structural patterns, including wave-like component arrangements.
The founders previously created AlphaChip, an earlier AI system designed for chip engineering.
AlphaChip generated an unexpected circular, donut-shaped component layout that surprised human engineers.
Despite early skepticism from engineers, the donut-shaped layout ultimately proved successful.
The platform's continuous learning mechanism improves layout efficiency with every design cycle.
Automating the physical layout process can shorten overall design time to about one week.

Background:
* Traditional chip floorplanning requires extensive manual placement and routing by human engineers.
* Partitioning silicon into distinct sub-blocks simplifies human verification but limits global component optimization.
* Modern semiconductor manufacturing relies on capital-intensive photolithography steps to fabricate silicon wafers.

Accelerated turnaround cycles will allow engineering teams to iterate physical hardware in days.
Specialized industries may no longer face prohibitive non-recurring engineering costs for custom silicon.

## 5. LFM2.5-24B-A2B: Built for fast on-device agents

### Overview

- Liquid AI has announced the release of LFM2.5-24B-A2B.
- It is a 24-billion-parameter reasoning model featuring 2 billion active parameters.
- The model is built specifically for fast and private on-device agents.
- It represents the largest and final entry in the LFM2.5 model family.
- The family now spans two orders of magnitude from LFM2.5-230M to LFM2.5-24B-A2B.
- The system operates strictly as a reasoning model producing explicit chains of thought.
- The reasoning approach leverages high decode speeds to improve final answer quality.
- Both base and post-trained versions are open-weight and available immediately.

### What happened

- Liquid AI launched LFM2.5-24B-A2B alongside its base variant, LFM2.5-24B-A2B-Base.
- Both models were released on Hugging Face and the Liquid AI Playground.
- Documentation was provided for running and fine-tuning the model locally.
- Liquid AI demonstrated the model running inside Hermes Agent on an Apple M5 Max.
- The demo executed planning and tool calls entirely on-device without cloud APIs.
- The tool-dispatch loop completed full cycles in well under one second per dispatch.
- Paid cloud API access was made available through Liquid Console.
- New Liquid Console accounts receive two dollars in starting credit.
- Cloud API pricing is set at ten cents per million input tokens.
- Cached input tokens are priced at five cents per million tokens.
