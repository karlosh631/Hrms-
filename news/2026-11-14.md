## 1. FAA lifts nationwide ground stop that blocked takeoffs for hours

### Overview

A communications problem shut down takeoffs across the US on Friday afternoon.

The national ground stop lasted for more than four hours before being lifted.

The FAA issued the nationwide ground stop at 3:44PM ET.

The stop was enacted due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety.

Officials announced the ground stop was lifted at 8:15PM ET, allowing flights to resume.

---

### What happened

Takeoffs from airports throughout the US largely stopped on Friday following the FAA order.

The outage began with the Chicago air traffic control center, according to Jon Ostrower.

Centers in Boston, Washington, New York, and Minneapolis followed the initial disruption.

As the FAA issued a ground stop for aircraft flying east, more facilities reported problems.

The issues disrupted both the radar and radio frequencies used by controllers and pilots.

Officials confirmed the ground stop was lifted after more than four hours.

---

### Why it matters

It remains unclear how many flights will be able to take off and when.

Airports are now facing problems of overloaded facilities and stranded planes.

Flight crews have run out of time to legally operate the planes.

Many people are currently stuck where they are for the time being.

More flights will inevitably face delays or cancellations as a result.

FlightAware listed 9,124 US flights as delayed by 8:39PM ET.
