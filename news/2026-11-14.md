## 1. FAA lifts nationwide ground stop that blocked takeoffs for hours

### Overview

A communications problem shut down takeoffs across the US on Friday afternoon.

The national ground stop lasted for more than four hours before being lifted.

The FAA issued the nationwide ground stop at 3:44PM ET.

The stop was enacted due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety.

Officials announced the ground stop was lifted at 8:15PM ET, allowing flights to resume.

---

### What happened

Takeoffs from airports throughout the US largely stopped on Friday following the FAA order.

The outage began with the Chicago air traffic control center, according to Jon Ostrower.

Centers in Boston, Washington, New York, and Minneapolis followed the initial disruption.

As the FAA issued a ground stop for aircraft flying east, more facilities reported problems.

The issues disrupted both the radar and radio frequencies used by controllers and pilots.

Officials confirmed the ground stop was lifted after more than four hours.

---

### Why it matters

It remains unclear how many flights will be able to take off and when.

Airports are now facing problems of overloaded facilities and stranded planes.

Flight crews have run out of time to legally operate the planes.

Many people are currently stuck where they are for the time being.

More flights will inevitably face delays or cancellations as a result.

FlightAware listed 9,124 US flights as delayed by 8:39PM ET.

The same tracking site listed 5,901 flights canceled by that time.

Transportation Secretary Ryan Duffy blamed an aging air traffic control system.

Duffy stated he is pursuing funds to replace the outdated infrastructure.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin stated there is currently no reason to suspect a cyberattack.

Officials confirmed there is no sign of a national security threat.

There is also no connection to the recent Microsoft Outlook outage.

---

### Technical details

Duffy explained that TRACONs started having data inconsistencies.

The inconsistency occurred in radar data and airplane communications.

The devices that experienced issues were analog telecom devices.

Switches and routers got overloaded with data and began showing inconsistency.

An earlier CBS News report cited sources pointing to an L3Harris update.

The private communications network operated by L3Harris allegedly caused a system reboot and outage.

L3Harris has not yet responded to a request for comment from The Verge.

Duffy and Mullin confirmed that patches to the existing system sparked the issue.

Addressing the patches, Duffy defended the action by noting inaction injects way more risk.

Duffy admitted he did not anticipate an outage of this magnitude.

---

### Industry impact

CNN noted that the last nationwide ground stop occurred in 2023.

The 2023 ground stop lasted about 90 minutes after the NOTAM system went down.

The prior nationwide ground stop before 2023 happened after the attacks on September 11th, 2001.

Reporters asked about a possible connection to the SMART system.

The cloud-based AI Strategic Management of Airspace, Routes and Trajectories system launched nationwide on November 10th.

Air Space Intelligence designed the system under an $875 million contract awarded in June.

ASI denied any connection to the current ground stop via a tweet.

ASI stated the event is not based on or a result of the SMART system.

ASI also stated no product of theirs or integration with FAA and ATC caused it.

---

### What to watch next

Airports must clear out overloaded terminals and manage grounded aircraft.

Airlines must resolve scheduling issues caused by crews running out of operating time.

Officials continue investigating the exact sequence of the analog telecom device overload.

Further updates will clarify the timeline for full flight schedule recovery.

Background: The source text mentions historical ground stops in 2023 and 2001.

Background: L3Harris operates a private communications network for air traffic control systems.

Background: Air Space Intelligence was awarded a 12-year $875 million contract in June to deploy the SMART system.

What is not yet known is the precise total number of final flight cancellations and delays.

What is also not yet known is how long the aviation system will take to fully recover.

## 4. Check Point gateways hacked via PHP2Shell to deploy VMware ESXi wiper

### Overview

Today is November 14, 2026.

Vulnerable Check Point Security Gateway instances have been targeted in recent cyber attacks.

The threat actors leveraged PHP2Shell flaws to deploy data-wiping malware on VMware ESXi hosts.

Attacks began on October 30.

Initial targeting focused on Israeli organizations.

A single actor appears to have conducted the entire campaign.

This campaign spanned initial access, lateral movement, and the launching of PHP webshells.

Researchers observed political messages during the destructive stage of the attacks.

These messages have raised questions about a possible false-flag operation.

---

### What happened

Exploitation started when the attacker gained access through the gateways’ Mobile Access portal.

The attacker used the PHP2Shell vulnerabilities for this initial access.

Next, they exploited a newly disclosed zero-day vulnerability tracked as CVE-2026-123039 to gain root privileges.

These privileges were abused to install PHP webshells and the TSGA backdoor.

The TSGA backdoor embeds a networking component called TSProxy.

TSProxy listens for attacker commands concealed within regular traffic.

In attacks targeting Israeli organizations, actors moved from the gateway to VMware ESXi hosts.

They located these hosts using the gogo scanner, which is an open-source network reconnaissance tool.

In the final stage, the threat actor deployed a data wiper called destroy_v6.

This wiper recursively deletes data in the target directory.

A second wave of attacks occurred on November 2.

This second wave targeted companies in multiple countries and sectors.

According to researchers, the second wave did not include destructive activity.

However, the specific follow-on actions taken by threat actors during the second wave remain unclear.

---

### Why it matters

The attacks demonstrate how critical infrastructure and security gateways can be compromised.

Threat actors successfully chained vulnerabilities to escalate privileges and deploy destructive payloads.

The deployment of data-wiping malware directly impacts enterprise virtualization environments like VMware ESXi.

Political messages left on hosts complicate attribution efforts and hint at false-flag tactics.

The involvement of multiple severe vulnerabilities underscores the risks of unpatched edge devices.

---

### Technical details

PHP2Shell is the collective name for two critical PHP flaws.

These flaws are tracked as CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619.

They allow unauthenticated remote code execution on vulnerable systems.

PHP fixed these vulnerabilities in emergency patches on October 21.

Amazon Threat Intelligence noted that exploitation attempts actually started four days earlier, on October 17.

CISA added both issues to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on October 26.

CISA later warned that attackers were chaining PHP2Shell bugs with the Fragnesia Linux kernel flaw to gain root privileges.

The destroy_v6 data wiper first tries a zero fill to make file recovery harder.

It also supports a fast mode that skips the overwriting step.
