## 1. NY announces game ownership bill requiring ownership rather than licensing

### Overview
New York has joined legislative efforts to protect video game consumers.
Representative Ritchie Torres has introduced the Power To The Players Act.
Torres serves as the representative for New York's 15th congressional district.
The proposed federal legislation targets anti-consumer practices in the gaming market.
Under the proposed bill, buying a video game grants consumer ownership instead of a license.
Publishers would also be required to provide offline functionality before shutting down servers.
The legislation directs the Federal Trade Commission to establish formal game ownership regulations.

### What happened
Representative Ritchie Torres officially introduced the Power To The Players Act.
The bill directs the FTC to establish clear rules regarding video game ownership.
Purchasing a game would legally grant ownership rather than a revocable license.
Torres's announcement aims to curb prevalent anti-consumer business practices.
Games featuring online elements must include an offline mode before server shutdowns occur.
Torres specifically highlighted sports releases such as NBA 2K25 in his announcement.
NBA 2K25 is reportedly removed from digital storefronts with servers ending after two years.
This leaves players unable to access a primary feature of the game they bought.
New York joins Pennsylvania in pushing legislation to safeguard gamers' rights.
Pennsylvania state senator John Kane previously introduced the Protect Our Games Act.
Kane's bill requires publishers to provide an offline mode or community servers.
Alternatively, the Pennsylvania bill mandates a minimum refund of 25 percent.

### Why it matters

Despite higher prices, games frequently arrive with predetermined expiration dates.

They stated clicking "purchase" would not lead consumers to expect a durable right.
Sony characterized these purchases as revocable licenses or one-time rental fees.

### Technical details
Many titles require online server connections even for single-player campaign modes.
Ubisoft demonstrated this issue in 2024 when it shut down The Crew.
The Crew was an online-only racing game retired during server upkeep.
The shutdown rendered the game completely unplayable, even for recent purchasers.
Ubisoft provided neither an offline mode nor support for community servers.
The Crew's single-player campaign fully depended on connections to Ubisoft's servers.
Blizzard completely killed the original Overwatch to launch Overwatch 2.
Overwatch was replaced outright rather than remaining a separately playable product.
Annual sports franchises from EA, 2K, and Sony regularly deprecate server access.
The Stop Killing Games movement demands offline modes and peer-to-peer patching.
The movement also calls for publisher support for player-hosted community servers.
Digital rights management software often slows down game performance for legitimate buyers.

### Industry impact
Frustrated consumers have popularized the slogan: "If buying isn't owning, piracy isn't stealing."
Many players argue that piracy currently provides a superior user experience.
Pirated copies remove DRM that degrades performance.
Unauthorized copies also provide permanent versions that publishers cannot remotely break.
Many gamers state they would prefer purchasing games if official versions worked better.
The Stop Killing Games movement continues to challenge industry licensing models.

## 3. Hackers breach Microsoft 365 tenants via ConnectWise supply-chain attack

### Overview

ConnectWise has shut down customer access to its Cloud Backup service.
The shutdown follows a supply-chain attack compromising Microsoft 365 customer tenants.
The targeted platform was formerly known as SkyKick Cloud Backup.
Threat actors accessed application identities to compromise downstream client environments.
ConnectWise halted service access at 05:01 UTC on October 17.

### What happened

Reports of unauthorized tenant access began surfacing from administrators on Friday.
Administrators observed unauthorized access to client data tied to a potential SkyKick breach.
Cybersecurity platform Huntress confirmed identifying at least 162 compromised tenants.
Microsoft confirmed unauthorized authentication attempts originating from a specific IP address on October 15.
Scripted sign-in attempts tied to the attacker IP address were also detected on October 13 and 14.
An administrator reported that a SkyKick service principal assigned itself the Exchange Administrator role on October 15.
ConnectWise confirmed unauthorized access to application identities in the Cloud Backup environment.
The vendor shut down application access for its partners and clients.
ConnectWise has launched an investigation and is conducting remediation efforts.

### Why it matters

ConnectWise Cloud Backup protects emails and files for managed service providers and IT teams.
The service secures customer data across Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace.
The attackers bypassed standard security defenses like multi-factor authentication.
Because automated scripts authenticated using application secrets, multi-factor authentication was ineffective.
The compromised application held broad, pre-existing permissions across customer Microsoft 365 tenants.
These permissions gave attackers the technical ability to read and write emails and attachments.
The threat actors also gained the ability to access OneDrive files, SharePoint files, and Teams chats.
Microsoft Threat Intelligence characterized the incident as an active supply-chain attack.

### Technical details

The backup service relies on permissions granted through Microsoft Entra ID service principals.
The attacker authenticated directly to Microsoft 365 as the backup application using its secret.
Microsoft, Huntress, and Todyl all tracked the malicious activity to the IP address 144.172.64[.]128.
Attacks abused the legitimate Cloud Backup application ID 506e08f9-6e9a-4da4-942b-a099a03644ae.
Older tenants may list the implicated integrations under the name SkyKick Cloud Manager.
Automated scripts executed sign-in requests using the curl/7.88.1 user agent.
Investigators also identified user agents python-urllib/3.12, python-requests/2.31, and python-requests/2.34.2.
Microsoft assessed that attackers likely abused existing broad permissions, including Directory.ReadWrite.All.
Huntress observed ConnectWise Cloud Management apps assigning directory roles in nearly 50 monitored tenants.
The unauthorized directory assignments were mostly for the Exchange Administrator role.
In one tenant, Cloud Management - MS Teams with ID 24205314-2b89-4e07-a2a4-579e898a5640 assigned itself Global Administrator.
The attacker then added credentials to an MSP management tool and to Huntress's own application.
This persistence operation took approximately five minutes to complete.
Microsoft detected backdoor persistence followed by Microsoft Graph API reconnaissance and credential addition attempts.
In nine tenants analyzed by Todyl, researchers found sign-in activity without persistence or credential creation.

### Industry impact

The vendor's service shutdown does not remove credentials already planted inside customer tenants.
Huntress warned administrators to review secrets and certificates across all applications.
Admins must remove any unauthorized credential entries added since October 13.
Todyl warned that disabling an application stops new tokens, but existing tokens remain valid until expiration.
Revoking user sessions does not terminate application-based access tokens.
ConnectWise advised administrators to inspect Entra service principal sign-ins for unexpected IP addresses.
Administrators should filter sign-in logs using the specific Cloud Backup application ID.
Microsoft recommended reviewing application credential changes, OAuth grants, and related Graph activity.
Huntress advised administrators to disable the implicated ConnectWise applications.
ConnectWise stated it has no indicators to date that partner backup data has been compromised.
The vendor stated that existing cloud backup archives remain intact and unaffected.

### What to watch next

ConnectWise has not yet provided a timeline for restoring Cloud Backup connections.
The full number of compromised customer tenants remains unclear.
The total extent of data theft across affected tenants has not yet been determined.
It remains unknown how the threat actor originally obtained the vendor's application secrets.

Background:
- Service principals act as software identities to access resources in Microsoft Entra ID.
- Cloud backup solutions require broad directory permissions to protect mailboxes and files.
- Application secrets allow automated tools to authenticate directly without human logins.
- Multi-factor authentication mechanisms protect interactive user logins, not service principal authentications.
- Supply-chain attacks targeting administrative platforms can expose hundreds of downstream client organizations.
- Organizations must actively audit directory logs for unauthorized role assignments and new application credentials.

## 5. Node.js creator ditches Deno to work on open source Cloudflare Workers

### Overview

Ryan Dahl, the creator of Node.js and Deno, is stepping away from traditional server runtimes.

Cloudflare has acquired Dahl's startup Deno in an acquihire transaction.

Dahl now argues that developers choosing Node.js to build servers are doing it wrong.

Deno will see its direct development wound down by Cloudflare after one year.

Dahl and his core team will instead focus on expanding Cloudflare's Workers ecosystem.

Their goal is to develop an open-source, highly efficient runtime for modern distributed applications.

### What happened

Cloudflare announced the acquisition of Deno and its engineering team last week.

The deal brings Dahl and 12 Deno colleagues over to Cloudflare.

Dahl originally created Deno as a modern alternative to his earlier creation, Node.js.

Venture firm Sequoia backed Deno with $21 million in funding in 2022.

By August, Deno had gathered around 400,000 monthly active users.

However, Dahl noted that Deno could not overcome the massive gravity of Node.js.

To gain adoption, Deno had to backpedal and become increasingly Node-compatible.

Deno will receive bug fixes and security patches from Cloudflare for only one year.

After that period, Cloudflare will completely end development of the runtime.

Dahl is currently searching for a new home for the MIT-licensed open source project.

He suggested the OpenJS Foundation as one possible candidate to adopt Deno.

### Why it matters

Dahl originally introduced Node.js in 2009 using Google's V8 JavaScript engine.

He created Node because traditional servers like Apache consumed excessive amounts of memory.

Node concurrently handled thousands of requests per second using minimal memory compared to Apache.

Today, Node powers approximately 30 million websites worldwide and has 130 million downloads.

Dahl launched Deno in 2018 to fix design flaws in Node.js.

Deno introduced native TypeScript support, a security permission model, and built-in fetch capabilities.

Despite those features, Deno remained trapped as an alternative Node implementation.

Dahl now contends that building servers at the Linux virtual machine layer is fundamentally flawed.

He believes Cloudflare Workers and isolate-based models represent the actual future of server-side computing.

### Technical details

Traditional server setups like Node and Deno run inside Linux virtual machines.

Node and Deno instances typically require hundreds of megabytes of memory each.

In August, Dahl released `celld`, an open-source implementation of Cloudflare Durable Objects.

Durable Objects were originally created at Cloudflare by Kenton Varda.
