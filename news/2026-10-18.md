## 1. NY announces game ownership bill requiring ownership rather than licensing

### Overview
New York has joined legislative efforts to protect video game consumers.
Representative Ritchie Torres has introduced the Power To The Players Act.
Torres serves as the representative for New York's 15th congressional district.
The proposed federal legislation targets anti-consumer practices in the gaming market.
Under the proposed bill, buying a video game grants consumer ownership instead of a license.
Publishers would also be required to provide offline functionality before shutting down servers.
The legislation directs the Federal Trade Commission to establish formal game ownership regulations.

### What happened
Representative Ritchie Torres officially introduced the Power To The Players Act.
The bill directs the FTC to establish clear rules regarding video game ownership.
Purchasing a game would legally grant ownership rather than a revocable license.
Torres's announcement aims to curb prevalent anti-consumer business practices.
Games featuring online elements must include an offline mode before server shutdowns occur.
Torres specifically highlighted sports releases such as NBA 2K25 in his announcement.
NBA 2K25 is reportedly removed from digital storefronts with servers ending after two years.
This leaves players unable to access a primary feature of the game they bought.
New York joins Pennsylvania in pushing legislation to safeguard gamers' rights.
Pennsylvania state senator John Kane previously introduced the Protect Our Games Act.
Kane's bill requires publishers to provide an offline mode or community servers.
Alternatively, the Pennsylvania bill mandates a minimum refund of 25 percent.

### Why it matters
Video games are currently more expensive than ever before.
Despite higher prices, games frequently arrive with predetermined expiration dates.
Aggressive in-game monetization schemes have faced intense worldwide player scrutiny.
Consumers expect to enjoy games even after publishers discontinue official support.
Players are pushing back against paying premium prices for temporary access.
Sony has argued that players do not actually own their purchased games.
Sony asserted its legal right to revoke game access whenever it benefits the company.
In an ongoing class-action lawsuit defense, Sony lawyers addressed digital purchases directly.
They stated clicking "purchase" would not lead consumers to expect a durable right.
Sony characterized these purchases as revocable licenses or one-time rental fees.

### Technical details
Many titles require online server connections even for single-player campaign modes.
Ubisoft demonstrated this issue in 2024 when it shut down The Crew.
The Crew was an online-only racing game retired during server upkeep.
The shutdown rendered the game completely unplayable, even for recent purchasers.
Ubisoft provided neither an offline mode nor support for community servers.
The Crew's single-player campaign fully depended on connections to Ubisoft's servers.
Blizzard completely killed the original Overwatch to launch Overwatch 2.
Overwatch was replaced outright rather than remaining a separately playable product.
Annual sports franchises from EA, 2K, and Sony regularly deprecate server access.
The Stop Killing Games movement demands offline modes and peer-to-peer patching.
The movement also calls for publisher support for player-hosted community servers.
Digital rights management software often slows down game performance for legitimate buyers.

### Industry impact
Frustrated consumers have popularized the slogan: "If buying isn't owning, piracy isn't stealing."
Many players argue that piracy currently provides a superior user experience.
Pirated copies remove DRM that degrades performance.
Unauthorized copies also provide permanent versions that publishers cannot remotely break.
Many gamers state they would prefer purchasing games if official versions worked better.
The Stop Killing Games movement continues to challenge industry licensing models.
The movement pushes back against selling games as temporary, revocable licenses.
Federal legislation could force publishers to fundamentally adjust their business practices.

### What to watch next
Background:
- Concrete definitions and detailed provisions of the bill have not yet been released.
- It is not yet known whether transfer rights mentioned by Torres apply to digital purchases.
- Transfer rights are expected to be particularly relevant for physical disc copies.
- The legislation must strictly define what legal ownership means for consumers.
- The bill must clarify what rights buyers retain over their software copies.
- It remains to be seen how effectively the legislation will execute its stated goals.
- Observers are monitoring whether the FTC will establish enforceable video game regulations.

## 2. Replit makes OpenAI its default over Anthropic, CEO says

### Overview

Replit has officially made OpenAI its default model provider over Anthropic.
The AI coding platform has become a favorite of startup founders and developers.
Replit CEO and co-founder Amjad Masad announced the shift onstage at TechCrunch Disrupt.
Masad commented on the partnership shift on Thursday during his Disrupt presentation.
OpenAI's ChatGPT now serves as the default model powering Replit Agent.
Masad stated that the decision was based on cost, partnership, and performance.
The platform has not eliminated Anthropic models entirely from its software development environment.
Customers still have the option to manually select Anthropic models for their projects.

### What happened

Masad initially stated onstage Thursday that Replit was no longer working with Anthropic.
Replit had previously announced a partnership with OpenAI in August.
At the time of that August announcement, Replit gave no indication of leaving Anthropic.
After the TechCrunch Disrupt event, Masad clarified Replit's relationship with Anthropic.
Masad clarified that Anthropic models remain available to users across the platform.
Masad stated over the weekend that Replit remains fundamentally model agnostic.
Users can still select Anthropic's Claude or other supported models inside Replit.
However, OpenAI is now the preferred partner powering Replit Agent.
Insiders familiar with the partnership disputed Masad’s onstage remarks about cutting ties.
One person familiar with the matter stated that the Anthropic partnership is still in place.
Replit was promoting Anthropic products across its website and social media recently.
A late September blog post showed Claude Fable 5 and 5.1 in Replit Agent production.
Around that time, a Replit product employee posted on X promoting Anthropic's Opus 5.5 model.
TechCrunch originally published the report on October 16 at 2:22 pm PT.
TechCrunch updated the article to incorporate additional context about Replit's AI partnerships.
TechCrunch also updated the article's headline following Masad's clarifications.

### Why it matters

Prior to the OpenAI agreement, Replit relied on Claude models to power its platform.
Replit was, until recently, a top-five customer of Anthropic.
Replit's prior reliance on Claude models had helped significantly grow its corporate revenue.
Making ChatGPT the default will likely reduce Replit’s ongoing spend with Anthropic.
Most platform customers stick with default model selections rather than manually picking alternatives.
Cost considerations played a direct role in Replit's decision to name OpenAI as default.
Masad cited internal benchmarks and stated that he thinks ChatGPT is better now.
Masad noted that OpenAI was viewed as a better partner for now.
The decision highlights intense competition between OpenAI and Anthropic for major developer platforms.
