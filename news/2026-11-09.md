## 2. Outlook.com bounces legit emails as Microsoft's authentication crackdown goes sideways

### Overview
Today is November 9, 2026, and Microsoft is facing scrutiny over email delivery issues.
A validation error has recently stopped Outlook.com from accepting legitimate emails.
The problem appears widespread across Microsoft's consumer email services.
Affected users have reported receiving non-delivery reports with specific error codes.
These error codes are related to SPF validation failures.
Other users noticed errors highlighting a lack of DMARC information.
The Register reported on these authentication-related email bounces.

### What happened
The troubles began at the end of last week.
They appear to result from Microsoft attempting to tighten email acceptance rules.
If a domain has missing DMARC or wobbly SPF, Outlook.com treats it as suspicious.
Consequently, emails from those domains are not accepted.
Small senders now appear to be affected by the crackdown.
Complainants in the Outlook forum note that even a single email with one line can be rejected.
Microsoft has not issued any customer advisory explaining how the rules changed.
The glitch also appears to be intermittent.
A resend attempt might sometimes succeed following a rejection.
Rejection error messages can be vague about what caused the bounce.
A Microsoft support forum moderator acknowledged the bug.
The moderator stated the issue was classified as a service degradation.
Microsoft is actively investigating the underlying cause of the delivery issue.
Engineering teams are analyzing service telemetry and customer-provided data.

### Why it matters
The rules are officially designed to deal with spam and bulk senders.
In 2025, Microsoft announced rules to strengthen email authentication for high-volume senders.
Those rules targeted domains sending more than 5,000 emails per day.
However, everyday users and small senders are now getting caught in the crossfire.
Some customers insist their settings were compliant all along.
Many users report that setting preferences to Microsoft's liking makes email flow again.
The Windows giant has not responded to inquiries from journalists regarding the glitch.

### Technical details
SPF must pass for the sending domain.
The domain's DNS record must accurately list authorized IP addresses and hosts.
DKIM must pass to validate email integrity and authenticity.
DMARC must have at least p=none.
DMARC must also align with either SPF or DKIM, preferably both.
In 2025, DKIM compliance was also required for domains sending over 5,000 emails daily.

### Industry impact
Earlier today, users in Europe, the Middle East, and Africa faced a separate Microsoft incident.
A recent service change caused some connections to be abruptly terminated.
Outlook on the web, Purview, and Defender portals were all affected by that separate issue.
Microsoft reverted the change in the Exchange Online front-end infrastructure to fix it.
That separate whoopsie was resolved, but email bouncing issues continue to puzzle users.

### What to watch next
Microsoft has yet to explain what else might have changed in its email services.
It remains unconfirmed what exact changes triggered legitimate messages to bounce back.
The company is still analyzing customer-provided data to identify the exact source of the bug.
Observers await an official technical advisory from Microsoft regarding the tightened authentication rules.
Affected administrators continue monitoring their domain DMARC, SPF, and DKIM settings closely.

## 3. Tesla expands robotaxi service to San Antonio

### Overview

Tesla is expanding its robotaxi service to another Texas city.

The company aims to compete with Waymo in the autonomous vehicle market.

Tesla also wants to position itself as more than just an automaker.

The announcement was made via a post on the X platform.


### What happened

Tesla announced that its robotaxis will launch in San Antonio.

The service is scheduled to begin at 5 a.m. local time on Monday.

Tesla did not provide additional operational details in its announcement.

Unanswered details include hours of operation and specific vehicle models.

It remains unclear if the fleet will use gold, two-seater Cybcercabs.

It is also unknown if modified Model Y vehicles will be used.

Tesla did not state whether human safety monitors will be on board.

Following previous launch playbooks, the rollout will likely start small.

A limited number of vehicles and a confined service area are expected.


### Why it matters

CEO Elon Musk promised autonomous vehicles for a decade.

Tesla eventually launched its robotaxi service in Austin in June 2025.

That initial launch was limited and used human safety operators on board.

It marked an important milestone for testing cameras and end-to-end AI.

In January, Tesla removed safety monitors from its Austin robotaxis.

That move kicked off a true driverless service for the company.

Tesla has spent the last 11 months expanding within Austin.

The company has also expanded to new cities in Florida and Texas.


### Technical details

Tesla operates robotaxis in limited areas of Miami and Orlando.

The company also serves Dallas, Fort Worth, and Houston.

Tesla has more than 400 autonomous vehicles registered in Austin.

Other city fleets range between a handful and a few dozen vehicles.

Texas registration data and Robotaxi Tracker provide these fleet numbers.

Fort Worth has just 24 Tesla vehicles according to the tracker.

Miami currently has only five vehicles listed in the tracker.

Tesla also operates a ride-hailing service in the San Francisco Bay Area.

This San Francisco service runs under a transportation charter permit.

Those vehicles feature a human driver behind the wheel.

Tesla lacks required permits for driverless ride-hailing in California.

Tesla released data showing fewer paying customer miles in Q2 than Q1.


### Industry impact

Rival Waymo also operates a robotaxi service in San Antonio.

Waymo uses a mix of driverless Jaguar I-Pace and Zeekr RT vehicles.

Waymo brands its vehicles as Ojai in its operating cities.

Waymo operates in Austin, Dallas, Houston, and San Antonio.

Waymo recently restarted its San Antonio service after a five-month pause.

That pause followed incidents where vehicles got stuck in flooded roadways.


### What to watch next

Background: Tesla previously launched services in Austin, Miami, Orlando, Dallas, Fort Worth, and Houston.

Background: Waymo operates competing services using Jaguar I-Pace and Zeekr RT vehicles branded as Ojai.

What is not yet known is the exact vehicle count for the San Antonio fleet.

It is not yet known if Cybercabs or modified Model Ys will be deployed.

It remains unconfirmed if human safety monitors will ride in the San Antonio vehicles.

Future operating hours and service area boundaries for San Antonio are still undisclosed.

## 4. Linus Torvalds sees signs AI-prompted patch flood has peaked

### Overview

Today is November 9, 2026, bringing fresh insights from the open-source world.

Linus Torvalds has shared his weekly kernel update post with the community.

The Linux kernel development leader has officially delivered Linux 7.4 rc2.

This release represents the second rough cut of the next kernel version.

Torvalds rated this latest release as a big one for two distinct reasons.

### What happened

Torvalds noted that he left some code out of rc1.

He made this omission because he was busy during the cycle.

It was already the largest such release in the history of the kernel.

The second reason involves what Torvalds described as “various AI fixes.”

Such fixes became necessary due to a surge in bug-finding tools.

Anthropic launched its Mythos bug-finding model back in April.

This tool enabled developers to find more software flaws than previously possible.

Torvalds’ latest post suggests the worst of this patchpocalypse may be behind us.

He highlighted comments from Alexei Starovoitov regarding the bpf side.

Alexei claimed that fixes are now arriving faster than new bug reports there.

Torvalds called that development lovely if the claim proves to be true.

### Why it matters

AI-prompted bug finding created a massive flood of software patches.

Microsoft addressed a record 974 flaws during the month of September alone.

This marked Microsoft's third record fixing spree of the current year.

The tech giant previously whacked 622 flaws in July and 206 in June.

Oracle also addressed a massive 1,449 flaws in a single month.

These numbers represent far more patches than IT teams are set up to handle.

Organizations must find ways to deal with bugs more quickly now.

Attackers are increasingly targeting software flaws originally found by AI.

If the patch deluge peaks, it brings welcome news for developers and users.
