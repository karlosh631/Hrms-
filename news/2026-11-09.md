## 2. Outlook.com bounces legit emails as Microsoft's authentication crackdown goes sideways

### Overview
Today is November 9, 2026, and Microsoft is facing scrutiny over email delivery issues.
A validation error has recently stopped Outlook.com from accepting legitimate emails.
The problem appears widespread across Microsoft's consumer email services.
Affected users have reported receiving non-delivery reports with specific error codes.
These error codes are related to SPF validation failures.
Other users noticed errors highlighting a lack of DMARC information.
The Register reported on these authentication-related email bounces.

### What happened
The troubles began at the end of last week.
They appear to result from Microsoft attempting to tighten email acceptance rules.
If a domain has missing DMARC or wobbly SPF, Outlook.com treats it as suspicious.
Consequently, emails from those domains are not accepted.
Small senders now appear to be affected by the crackdown.
Complainants in the Outlook forum note that even a single email with one line can be rejected.
Microsoft has not issued any customer advisory explaining how the rules changed.
The glitch also appears to be intermittent.
A resend attempt might sometimes succeed following a rejection.
Rejection error messages can be vague about what caused the bounce.
A Microsoft support forum moderator acknowledged the bug.
The moderator stated the issue was classified as a service degradation.
Microsoft is actively investigating the underlying cause of the delivery issue.
Engineering teams are analyzing service telemetry and customer-provided data.

### Why it matters
The rules are officially designed to deal with spam and bulk senders.
In 2025, Microsoft announced rules to strengthen email authentication for high-volume senders.
Those rules targeted domains sending more than 5,000 emails per day.
However, everyday users and small senders are now getting caught in the crossfire.
Some customers insist their settings were compliant all along.
Many users report that setting preferences to Microsoft's liking makes email flow again.
The Windows giant has not responded to inquiries from journalists regarding the glitch.

### Technical details
SPF must pass for the sending domain.
