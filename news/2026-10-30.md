## 1. PC chip plunge wipes out a quarter of Intel’s value

### Overview

Intel suffered a steep stock collapse after releasing its latest quarterly results.
The company saw its share price plunge by nearly twenty-four percent.
A sudden downturn in personal computer processor sales sparked investor panic.
The sell-off erased nearly a quarter of Intel's total market valuation.

CEO Lip Bu Tan warned that the PC processor market faces prolonged challenges.
Overall revenue reached sixteen point five billion dollars despite the PC slump.
Manufacturing supply shortages are preventing Intel from fulfilling surging datacenter demand.
The company is attempting to pivot manufacturing lines toward server products.

### What happened

Intel reported third-quarter revenue of sixteen point five billion dollars.
This represented a twenty-one percent increase year over year.
The revenue result landed at the high end of Intel's guidance range.
Intel had previously projected revenue between fifteen point eight and sixteen point eight billion dollars.

Gross margins reached forty-three percent for the quarter.
Gross margins beat the company's internal guidance by two percentage points.
Datacenter and artificial intelligence product revenues surged by seventy-eight percent.
Conversely, client computing and physical AI generated eight point one billion dollars.

Client computing revenue declined five percent compared to the prior year.
Client revenue fell nine percent compared to the preceding quarter.
Intel stock traded at ninety-two dollars and ninety-six cents at market open.
The stock dropped immediately to seventy-three dollars following the earnings announcement.
After-hours trading drove the share price down to seventy dollars and eighty-seven cents.
The drop represented a total share price decline of twenty-three point seven percent.

### Why it matters

Client computing represents Intel's largest individual business segment.
Rising system end-point prices have placed serious pressure on corporate IT budgets.
Higher memory costs and limited component availability have slowed PC sales.
Hardware manufacturing partners are aggressively reducing their chip inventory.

Lip Bu Tan predicted the PC processor market will shrink twelve percent next year.
The PC market is expected to struggle until memory supply normalizes.
South Korean memory maker SK Hynix warned memory shortages could last until 2030.
Intel is simultaneously facing severe constraints in sourcing raw silicon wafers.

Chief financial officer Dave Zinsner declined to offer server chip revenue guidance.
Zinsner warned Intel will likely not fully meet customer server processor demand.
Supply difficulties will be especially severe during the first half of the coming year.

### Technical details

Intel reported a strong resurgence in customer demand for central processing units.
Energy efficiency has become the primary metric in artificial intelligence deployments.
CEO Tan explained that GPUs specialize in reasoning or thinking tasks.
A single GPU reasoning query can consume multiple watt-hours of power.

In contrast, deterministic CPUs handle finding tasks far more efficiently.
A CPU-based query requires only joules of energy to execute.
Low-power computing is crucial for emerging agentic artificial intelligence systems.
Agentic AI relies on autonomous software agents performing personal and professional tasks.

Intel projects the server CPU total addressable market will increase fivefold.
The server CPU market could expand from forty billion dollars to two hundred billion dollars.
This market growth is predicted to occur over the next five years.
Intel is moving manufacturing capacity across its fabs to prioritize datacenter silicon.
The company is actively converting some PC processor capacity to datacenter chip production.

### Industry impact

Original equipment manufacturers are reducing orders to cope with slower retail demand.
Persistent memory shortages threaten long-term recovery for the entire PC market.
Intel is pushing process improvements to reach manufacturing parity with TSMC.
Achieving parity with TSMC is expected to strengthen Intel's third-party foundry services.

Intel is experiencing shortages of essential manufacturing materials like silicon wafers.
Datacenter customers face constrained supply for energy-efficient server CPUs.

### What to watch next

Investors will watch whether Intel can secure enough silicon wafers next year.
Analysts will monitor Intel's fab shifts as capacity moves to datacenter silicon.
The industry will track memory pricing and shortages through the 2030 timeline.
Market watchers will observe enterprise adoption of agentic AI workloads.

Intel's financial performance will be closely evaluated during a difficult first half.
Observers will monitor the multiyear turnaround effort led by CEO Lip Bu Tan.
Background: Intel has spent eighteen months restructuring its workforce and leadership.
Background: The chipmaker eliminated middle management layers to restore an engineering-centric culture.
Background: It is not yet known precisely when silicon wafer supply bottlenecks will ease.
Background: It remains unconfirmed how much server CPU demand will be left unmet.

## 2. Binance probes potential $5.8 billion crypto-theft hack

### Overview

Cryptocurrency exchange Binance is investigating a potential major security breach.
The investigation involves the possible theft of up to $5.8 billion in digital assets.
If confirmed, this would become the single largest cryptocurrency hack in history.
Stablecoin issuers have already acted by freezing more than $2.3 billion in assets.
A Binance spokesperson told BleepingComputer that user funds remain completely safe.
Binance confirmed its internal teams are actively investigating the security issue.
The exchange pledged to release verified information as soon as it becomes available.

### What happened

Blockchain analysts detected billions in cryptocurrency leaving Binance hot wallets.
The assets were transferred directly to a newly created Ethereum address.
Crypto journalist StarPlatinum reported that hot wallet "Binance 14" moved $2.53 billion.
That initial transaction transferred funds across 64 different cryptocurrency tokens.
Just 24 seconds later, a second Binance wallet transferred $174.4 million.
A third Binance hot wallet then transferred 780 million USDC to the same address.
These documented hot wallet transfers brought the reported total to roughly $3.48 billion.
Binance founder Changpeng Zhao publicly addressed the incident on X.
Zhao noted that the situation appeared to be a hack.
Zhao reminded the public that he no longer manages the exchange.
Zhao stated that he expects all user funds to be fully covered.

### Why it matters

A theft of this scale would dwarf all previous cryptocurrency cyberattacks.
It far exceeds the previous record theft of $1.5 billion from Bybit.
That Bybit incident was carried out by North Korean hackers in February 2025.
An unauthorized transfer of $5.8 billion poses massive financial and operational questions.
The event demonstrates how rapidly centralized stablecoin issuers can intervene during crises.
Hot wallet security remains one of the largest vulnerabilities facing digital asset exchanges.

### Technical details

Blockchain explorer Etherscan labeled the recipient address as "Binance Hot Wallet Exploiter 2."
Etherscan assigned both "Exploit" and "Heist" tags to this receiving address.
Intelligence firm Arkham Intelligence is monitoring 55 cryptocurrency addresses linked to the incident.
Arkham labeled the cluster of addresses under the entity "binance-hacker-1029."
Combined holdings across these 55 addresses total approximately $5.78 billion.
The tracked holdings include approximately $1.77 billion in Tether (USDT).
The addresses also contain approximately $1.44 billion worth of Bitcoin.
Roughly $876 million in Ethereum is stored across the tracked addresses.
The addresses further contain approximately $823 million in USD Coin (USDC).
Arkham noted that the $5.78 billion represents tracked assets rather than confirmed stolen funds.

Background:
* Cryptocurrency exchanges rely on hot wallets connected to the internet for daily liquidity.
* Hot wallets enable fast processing of customer deposits, trades, and withdrawal requests.
* Private keys controlling hot wallets must be secured against unauthorized access and software exploits.
* Most major exchanges store the bulk of client reserves in offline cold storage.
* Cold storage wallets remain disconnected from the internet to prevent remote compromise.
* Public blockchains allow independent analysts and firms to inspect transaction flows in real time.

### Industry impact

Stablecoin issuers moved aggressively to freeze funds transferred to the suspected hacker.
Tether CEO Paolo Ardoino announced on X that over $1.567 billion in USDT was frozen.
Circle CEO Jeremy Allaire confirmed that Circle froze more than $800 million in USDC.
Together, Tether and Circle froze more than $2.3 billion in stablecoin assets.
Allaire confirmed that Circle is actively coordinating with law enforcement agencies.
Circle stated it will continue supporting the ongoing investigation into the breach.
Binance advised its global user base to remain alert to phishing scams.
The exchange warned users to avoid unofficial links, unsolicited messages, and credential requests.

### What to watch next

Binance has not yet verified whether the transactions were completely unauthorized.
The exchange has not confirmed the root cause or mechanism behind the incident.
The total value of assets definitively stolen has not yet been confirmed by Binance.
Security teams continue reviewing data to verify the full scope of the breach.
Official announcements are expected through verified Binance communication channels.
Observers are tracking law enforcement efforts regarding the frozen stablecoin assets.
Analysts are watching whether the threat actor attempts to move Bitcoin or Ethereum funds.

Background: What is not yet known:
* The exact threat group or individual behind the transfers has not been identified.
* It remains unknown whether API keys, internal credentials, or software vulnerabilities were exploited.
* Binance has not detailed what proportion of its total reserves these transfers represent.
* The timeline for legal seizure and restitution of frozen assets remains undetermined.
* Investigators have not disclosed whether any international law enforcement agency has taken the lead.
* It is unknown if other centralized exchanges received any routed illicit funds.
* Official statements have not specified whether withdrawal halts or system maintenance will occur.

## 3. GM reaches $220M settlement over selling drivers’ private data

### Overview

General Motors has agreed to a $220 million multistate settlement.
The settlement resolves allegations that GM illegally sold drivers' private data.
A coalition of attorneys general from 36 states brought the lawsuit.
The data involved private driving behavior and vehicle location information.
GM sold this customer information directly to third-party data brokers.
Those data brokers subsequently resold the records to commercial auto insurers.

### What happened

The $220 million agreement was officially announced on Thursday.
State attorneys general alleged GM unlawfully monetized sensitive customer vehicle data.
The legal fallout traces back to a report by The New York Times.
The New York Times revealed GM and OnStar collected and sold geolocation data.
The investigation showed driver behavior was also tracked and commercialized.
Data brokers named in the reporting included LexisNexis and Verisk.
GM gathered this information through its Smart Driver program.
Smart Driver was a free feature inside GM's connected vehicle apps.
The program tracked and rated driving behavior alongside seat belt use.
Data brokers sold these driving metrics directly to insurance providers.
Insurers used the purchased information to adjust customer insurance rates.
GM discontinued the Smart Driver feature in 2024.
Terminating the program did not halt ongoing state and federal investigations.
The Federal Trade Commission finalized an enforcement order against GM in January.
The FTC order banned GM and OnStar from sharing specific consumer data.

### Why it matters

GM must now obtain express driver consent before collecting or sharing driving data.
Express consent is also required before sharing precise vehicle location data.
These requirements align with previous agreements GM reached with California and the FTC.
A new requirement mandates that GM submit to third-party privacy assessments.
The independent assessor will evaluate GM's compliance with its privacy program.
GM addressed the settlement in an emailed corporate statement.
GM stated that the agreement addresses the discontinued Smart Driver product.
The company said the settlement reinforces steps taken to strengthen privacy practices.
GM said connected vehicle data enables safer, helpful, and personalized experiences.
The automaker pledged clarity about what data is used and how it is protected.
GM also promised to be transparent about customer data-sharing choices.
The company confirmed it will no longer sell individual-linked vehicle data.
GM will not sell data tied to an individual, their location, or driving habits.
GM may still share anonymized data that is not tied to an individual.
The automaker updated the language in its public privacy policy.
GM also established a dedicated consumer-facing Privacy Center.

### Technical details

Connected car applications served as the primary collection mechanism for driver metrics.
The Smart Driver tool continuously monitored driving behaviors and seat belt usage.
Vehicle telematics were transmitted through GM's OnStar connected platform.
Data brokers ingested driver telemetry to compile risk profiles for insurers.
GM's revised policy restricts external data sharing to unlinked, anonymized formats.
Independent auditors will inspect GM's technical privacy systems under the agreement.
Modern sensor-laden vehicles increasingly feature significant onboard computing power.
Software-centric vehicles now regularly interface with user calendars, messages, and email.

### Industry impact

Automakers face escalating regulatory scrutiny over how they handle vehicle data.
State coalitions and federal agencies are enforcing rigorous explicit-consent mandates.
Car manufacturers can no longer quietly monetize driving habits through data brokers.
Vehicle software platforms must clearly separate identifiable driver data from anonymized feeds.
Third-party privacy compliance assessments may become standard across the auto industry.

### What to watch next

GM is rolling out a personal AI assistant for its OnStar platform.
OnStar is GM's 30-year-old safety and roadside assistance service.
Vehicle cameras will connect to the software to provide live video views.
Connected cameras will also record footage during crashes and safety incidents.
Drivers will be able to control vehicle climate settings using voice commands.
Users can also use voice to ask questions about specific vehicle features.
GM showcased the AI assistant during its GM Forward event in October.
GM emphasized that camera recordings will be encrypted and carry time limits.
The automaker stated that neither GM nor OnStar can access Connected Camera recordings.
Customers will be given software settings to manage their data permissions.
GM noted that opting out of data sharing will likely limit access to certain features.

Background:
* Connected vehicles routinely generate telematics data to support modern digital features.
* Independent verification requirements are increasingly utilized in major corporate privacy decrees.
* It is not yet known which third party will evaluate GM's privacy program.
* It is not yet known which commercial partners will receive GM's anonymized data.

## 4. Reddit will force everyone to log in

### Overview

Reddit is shifting to a logged-in-only experience.
Chief Executive Officer Steve Huffman announced the policy update.
Reddit will begin requiring users to log in to browse content.
The announcement coincided with Reddit's Q3 2026 earnings release today.
The platform previously allowed open browsing for the last two decades.
Huffman called this a big change for the platform and its business.
