## 2. DeepSeek deep-sixed at all US federal civilian agencies

### Overview

The US Department of Homeland Security has banned DeepSeek across federal civilian agencies.
Agencies must remove all existing technology implementations tied to the Chinese artificial intelligence lab.
The order applies to DeepSeek products and third-party tools developed using them.
Cybersecurity experts argue the directive is excessively broad and practically unenforceable.
The mandate could increase agency software costs by limiting access to low-cost models.

### What happened

The Department of Homeland Security issued its directive to civilian agencies on Monday.
Agencies are instructed to ditch all DeepSeek and DeepSeek-affiliate products immediately.
The mandate explicitly extends to third-party services developed using DeepSeek implementations.
A Department of Homeland Security spokesperson did not immediately respond to inquiries.
US scrutiny intensified following the release of the DeepSeek-R1 model in early 2025.
Representative Darin LaHood and Representative Josh Gottheimer introduced restrictive legislation in February 2025.
Their No DeepSeek on Government Devices Act failed to advance out of committee.
The Defense Information Systems Agency began blocking DeepSeek on Pentagon networks in January 2025.
The US Navy, NASA, and the House of Representatives took steps to exile DeepSeek.
Several US state governments also enacted similar technology bans on their networks.

### Why it matters

Lawmakers labeled DeepSeek a national security threat due to alleged data collection practices.
Officials warned that user data could be captured and transferred to the Chinese government.
The civilian order follows earlier restrictions imposed by defense and legislative bodies.
American artificial intelligence companies, including Anthropic, may experience a commercial boon.
Anthropic and other domestic model creators are currently preparing to go public.
Chinese open-weight models have offered intense competition based on cost and capability.
Disallowing these models leaves agencies reliant on favored American model providers.
Taxpayers funding federal civilian agencies may ultimately bear higher procurement costs.

### Technical details

The Department of Homeland Security order lacks a precise definition for "developed using."
Compliance remains unclear for software vibe-coded using DeepSeek assistance.
AI gateways like OpenRouter make DeepSeek models accessible through standard application programming interfaces.
OpenRouter automatically selects and routes requests to the best available model provider.
Vercel AI Gateway also defaults to routing requests to the best fit provider.
Civilian agencies utilizing these gateways risk querying DeepSeek models inadvertently.
Security concerns regarding DeepSeek's iOS mobile application emerged after the release of DeepSeek-R1.
A December 2025 report cited plaintext data leaks within DeepSeek Android and iOS apps.
The report noted missing SSL pinning and connections to servers operated by Chinese firms.
Advertising software development kits frequently suffer from these types of mobile security flaws.
The order cites general risks regarding potential malware or vulnerabilities inside the models.
Katie Moussouris of Luta Security called the ban on third-party implementations impractical.
Moussouris stated that an AI software bill of materials cannot track developer coding tools.
Vendors cannot practically verify that underlying code never interacted with DeepSeek.
Moussouris noted that hosting DeepSeek models domestically in the US mitigates foreign server risks.
NIST testing revealed DeepSeek models were more vulnerable to prompt injection than US competitors.
NIST also observed that DeepSeek models were comparatively easier to jailbreak.
Jailbreak guardrails often create obstacles for security defenders inspecting systems.

### Industry impact

US artificial intelligence vendors could see reduced market competition across federal agencies.
Federal contractors face significant compliance hurdles certifying software provenance.
Organizations must carefully audit AI gateways to avoid unintentional DeepSeek usage.
Commercial vendors may struggle to satisfy sweeping software attestation requirements.
Open-weight model adoption within the federal government faces substantial regulatory pushback.

### What to watch next

Agencies await further administrative guidance defining prohibited third-party development methods.
Contractors will watch how software bill of materials standards address artificial intelligence tooling.
Federal auditors will evaluate whether gateway users actively constrain model selections.
Industry analysts will assess the economic impact on government artificial intelligence budgets.

## 3. Amazon is taking its biggest shot at Nvidia yet with new TorchNeuron dev tool

### Overview

Amazon Web Services has launched a developer tool named TorchNeuron.
The tool helps startups transition AI workloads to Amazon's Trainium chips.
This move represents Amazon's most aggressive challenge to Nvidia to date.
Nvidia has long enjoyed a software advantage in artificial intelligence.
TorchNeuron is built to integrate directly with the PyTorch framework.
Developers can migrate their applications with minimal configuration effort.
Amazon also wants to uncouple PyTorch from specific proprietary hardware.
The strategy targets both hardware costs and software developer choice.

### What happened

AWS announced the release of TorchNeuron on Monday.
The tool allows AI applications to run on Amazon's Trainium silicon.
Developers can port PyTorch applications by changing one configuration line.
Amazon confirmed that no full code rewrite is required for migration.
TorchNeuron is adapted directly from the standard PyTorch framework.
Engineers familiar with PyTorch can easily navigate the new tool.
Amazon also announced plans to contribute code upstream to PyTorch.
These upstream contributions aim to remove vendor-specific chip ties.
The company published details about the initiative in an official blog post.

### Why it matters

Nvidia is currently the default hardware provider for artificial intelligence apps.
Nvidia achieved this through years of engineering its CUDA software platform.
CUDA ensures that Nvidia hardware supports PyTorch applications seamlessly.
This deep integration effectively embedded Nvidia hardware by default.
Amazon notes that roughly 85 percent of AI applications utilize PyTorch.
