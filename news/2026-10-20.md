## 2. DeepSeek deep-sixed at all US federal civilian agencies

### Overview

The US Department of Homeland Security has banned DeepSeek across federal civilian agencies.
Agencies must remove all existing technology implementations tied to the Chinese artificial intelligence lab.
The order applies to DeepSeek products and third-party tools developed using them.
Cybersecurity experts argue the directive is excessively broad and practically unenforceable.
The mandate could increase agency software costs by limiting access to low-cost models.

### What happened

The Department of Homeland Security issued its directive to civilian agencies on Monday.
Agencies are instructed to ditch all DeepSeek and DeepSeek-affiliate products immediately.
The mandate explicitly extends to third-party services developed using DeepSeek implementations.
A Department of Homeland Security spokesperson did not immediately respond to inquiries.
US scrutiny intensified following the release of the DeepSeek-R1 model in early 2025.
Representative Darin LaHood and Representative Josh Gottheimer introduced restrictive legislation in February 2025.
Their No DeepSeek on Government Devices Act failed to advance out of committee.
The Defense Information Systems Agency began blocking DeepSeek on Pentagon networks in January 2025.
The US Navy, NASA, and the House of Representatives took steps to exile DeepSeek.
Several US state governments also enacted similar technology bans on their networks.

### Why it matters

Lawmakers labeled DeepSeek a national security threat due to alleged data collection practices.
Officials warned that user data could be captured and transferred to the Chinese government.
