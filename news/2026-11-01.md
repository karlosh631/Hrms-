## 2. Amazon sends chills through its workforce with mixed message memo about AI use

### Overview

Amazon recently published an engineering treatise addressing developer roles in the age of AI.
The memo was written by Terk Terkowitz, a VP and Distinguished Engineer at the company.
Terkowitz is described as the leader of Amazon's technical contributor career track.
The communication was initially distributed internally to software engineers and managers in September.
Amazon made the treatise public during the final week of October 2026.
The treatise has sent chills through software engineers facing an increasingly bleak career outlook.
It provides contradictory guidance on whether engineers are required to use generative AI tools.
The publication coincides with reports of 250 additional job cuts at Amazon.

### What happened

Terkowitz published a detailed memo exploring how artificial intelligence alters software engineering.
The document attempts to make the future of the software engineering profession feel less bleak.
The memo states that if work does not benefit from AI tooling, that is a finding, not a failing.
Engineers are encouraged to document tool limitations and discuss them directly with their managers.
However, the memo also warns staff that non-adopters of AI tooling are falling behind.
It admits that input metrics are deeply flawed, yet advises managers to use them carefully anyway.
